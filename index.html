First visit?

Your first visit, step by step

The clinic sees patients by appointment only. Book first, then use the phrases and the symptom sheet below at the front desk.

  1. Book a time

    Book on the clinic's online booking site (in Japanese), by phone, or at the front desk. On the booking site, a first-time patient picks the menu starting with 当院初診 ("first visit") — the clinic asks you to choose this even if you only come for vaccines.

    Grooming, boarding, surgery, sedation and ultrasound can't be booked online. Call for these.

  2. Tell the front desk

    • Your pet's name
    • Type of animal and breed
    • Age
    • What's wrong, and since when

    Tip: show the symptom sheet below on your phone.

  3. What to bring

    • Vaccination records, if you have them
    • Records from your previous vet (US or Japan)
    • Any medicine your pet takes now
    • Your pet in a carrier or on a leash

    Payment: cash (yen), Visa or Mastercard, according to the clinic's website.

At the front desk

Five short phrases

Read the Japanese aloud, or just show this screen.

  • はじめてです。Hajimete desu.This is our first visit.
  • 予約した〇〇です。Yoyaku shita ___ desu.I have an appointment. My name is ___.
  • 予約していません。Yoyaku shite imasen.I don't have an appointment.
  • 〇日前から食べません。___ nichi mae kara tabemasen.He / she hasn't eaten for ___ days.
  • ワクチンを打ちたいです。Wakuchin o uchitai desu.I'd like a vaccination.

    Getting there

    About 5 minutes on foot from Keikyu Yokosuka-Chuo Station (route from the clinic's website).

    1. Leave Yokosuka-Chuo Station by the West exit.
    2. Walk up the hill (Hirasaka) with the More's shopping building on your right.
    3. At the first traffic light at the top (Uwamachi 1-chome police box), turn right.
    4. The clinic is about 100 m ahead on your left, on the ground floor.

    Driving: turn left just before the clinic, then right after the coin parking lot. The clinic's spaces are at the far end of the lot behind the building.

    Look for: glass doors under a white sign with a dog logo (as seen in the clinic's 2016 photo — the sign may have changed).

    FAQ

    Questions families ask

    Answers come from the clinic's website. Anything we could not confirm is marked “To be confirmed”.

    Do the staff speak English?

    To be confirmed

    Until this is confirmed, the phrases and symptom sheet on this page can help at the front desk.

    Can I pay by card?

    Yes: Visa and Mastercard, according to the clinic's website. Amex and JCB are not accepted right now, and neither are US dollars or e-money. Cash in yen is fine.

    Do I need an appointment?

    Yes, to see a vet. Book online, by phone or at the front desk. If the online calendar is full, call — the clinic asks you to phone in an emergency even when online booking is full.

    No appointment is needed for: nail trims, anal gland and ear cleaning (not for rabbits), picking up medicine or food, visiting a pet staying at the clinic, and pet-hotel drop-off or pick-up.

    The clinic also says it may not always be able to see a brand-new patient, depending on the case and the day's schedule. Booking ahead is the safest way.

    What about nights, Sundays and holidays?

    Sundays: open, same hours as weekdays. National holidays: morning only. Tuesdays: closed.

    Night and after-hours care: the clinic's website says it stopped offering this in September 2021.

    Which animals do they see?

    Dogs, cats, rabbits, ferrets and hamsters. The clinic does not see birds or reptiles.

    Can they fill out papers in English (for a PCS move)?

    To be confirmed

    The online booking site has a menu for a health certificate (健康診断証明書の発行). Ask the clinic about English versions.

    Is there parking?

    Yes, clinic spaces behind the building. Please park facing the wall. The clinic says space is limited, which is one reason visits are by appointment.
