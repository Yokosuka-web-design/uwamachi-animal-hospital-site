First visit?

Your first visit, step by step

The clinic sees patients by appointment only. Book first, then use the phrases and the symptom sheet below at the front desk.

  1. Book a time

    Book on the clinic's online booking site (in Japanese), by phone, or at the front desk. On the booking site, a first-time patient picks the menu starting with 当院初診 ("first visit") — the clinic asks you to choose this even if you only come for vaccines.

    Surgery and procedures that need general anesthesia can't be booked online. Call, or book at the front desk.

    First visit: the clinic asks you to fill in its online questionnaire before your appointment, or a paper one at the front desk. The form is in Japanese.

  2. Tell the front desk

    • Your pet's name
    • Type of animal and breed
    • Age
    • What's wrong, and since when

    Tip: show the symptom sheet below on your phone.

  3. What to bring

    • Vaccination records, if you have them
    • Records from your previous vet (US or Japan)
    • Any medicine your pet takes now
    • Your pet in a carrier or on a leash

    Payment: cash (yen), Visa or Mastercard, according to the clinic's website.

At the front desk

Five short phrases

Read the Japanese aloud, or just show this screen.

  • はじめてです。Hajimete desu.This is our first visit.
  • 予約した〇〇です。Yoyaku shita ___ desu.I have an appointment. My name is ___.
  • 予約していません。Yoyaku shite imasen.I don't have an appointment.
  • 〇日前から食べません。___ nichi mae kara tabemasen.He / she hasn't eaten for ___ days.
  • ワクチンを打ちたいです。Wakuchin o uchitai desu.I'd like a vaccination.

    Getting there

    5–6 minutes on foot from Keikyu Yokosuka-Chuo Station, and about 5 minutes by car from the Main Gate of Yokosuka Naval Base (from the clinic's website).

    1. Leave Yokosuka-Chuo Station by the West exit.
    2. Walk up the hill (Hirasaka) with the More's shopping building on your right.
    3. At the first traffic light at the top, turn right.
    4. The clinic is about 100 m ahead on your left, on the ground floor.

    Nearby: Oakiyama Park, at the bottom of the slope below Yokosuka Central Library.

    Driving: the clinic's 5 parking spaces are behind the building (photo on the right).

    FAQ

    Questions families ask

    Answers come from the clinic's website. Anything we could not confirm is marked “To be confirmed”.

    Does the vet speak English?

    Yes. The clinic's website says the vet speaks English and sees patients whose owners don't speak Japanese. The booking site and the questionnaire are in Japanese, so the guide on this page can help with those.

    Can I pay by card?

    Yes: Visa and Mastercard, according to the clinic's website. Amex and JCB are not accepted, and neither are e-money or other payment methods. Cash in yen is fine.

    Do I need an appointment?

    Yes, to see a vet — the clinic doesn't take walk-ins. Book online, by phone or at the front desk. If the online calendar is full, call the clinic.

    No appointment is needed for routine care such as nail trims and ear cleaning, picking up medicine or food, or visiting a pet staying at the clinic.

    Two pets: one booking can cover vaccinations for both. If they need different care, the clinic suggests booking each one.

    What if it's urgent?

    Call the clinic first, even if online booking shows full. The clinic's website says it fits in returning patients between appointments, and offers new patients the earliest open time — a same-day visit may not always be possible.

    What about nights, Sundays and holidays?

    Sundays: open, same hours as weekdays. National holidays: morning only. Tuesdays: closed.

    Nights and after hours: the clinic does not see patients outside its regular hours.

    Which animals do they see?

    Dogs, cats, rabbits, ferrets and hamsters. For other animals, ask the clinic first.

    Can they fill out papers in English (for a PCS move)?

    The clinic's English page says it can issue certificates in English, including for quarantine. The booking site also has a menu for a health certificate (健康診断証明書の発行). Ask the clinic which papers you need.

    Is there parking?

    Yes, 5 spaces behind the building (No. 1–5). Park head-in at No. 1–3. No. 4–5 are marked with traffic cones.

    If you're worried about your pet Please call the clinic first and ask: 046-821-3338.
